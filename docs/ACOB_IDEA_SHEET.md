---

## The core principle (non-negotiable)

The consumer always gets their **true choices**. Restaurants do **not** get to
influence what a user sees — not the ranking, not how many of their photos show
up, nothing. This is the ethical spine of the product and the thing that earns
long-term consumer trust. We monetize around it, never through it.

> Tempting idea to resist: letting restaurants pay to surface more of their
> photos, especially in the discovery phase. It would be lucrative, but it
> corrupts the one thing that makes ACOB trustworthy. Keep it walled off from
> the consumer experience.

## Revenue model

### Restaurant subscription (the current lead idea)

Charge restaurants a small flat fee to be on the platform.

- **Price point:** intentionally low — roughly **$2–$5 / month** per restaurant.
  Low enough to be a no-brainer, high enough to be hugely profitable at scale.
- **Land with a free trial:** put restaurants on for free initially, no friction.
- **Prove value with data:** during (and at the end of) the trial, show each
  restaurant a concrete number — *"Here's how many people we sent to your
  restaurant."* Attribution is the sales pitch.
- **Then convert:** *"If you'd like to continue with us, here's the cost."*
  Period. No pressure, no dark patterns — the traffic numbers do the selling.

**Why this works:**

- It's honest to the consumer — they still get unbiased, true choices. The
  restaurant paying us changes nothing about what the user sees.
- It's honest to the restaurant — they only pay once they've seen the value we
  delivered (measured foot traffic / referrals).
- At a $2–$5/mo price across a large base of restaurants, the margins are
  enormous while the per-restaurant cost stays trivial.

**Open questions to work through before the pitch:**

- How exactly do we measure and attribute "people we sent"? (Tap-to-directions,
  reservations, check-ins, redemption of something?) The credibility of the
  whole model rests on this number being real and defensible.
- Trial length and conversion mechanics.
- Tiering: is it truly flat, or is there a reason to have a second tier later
  (without ever letting money touch consumer-facing ranking)?
- Payment/billing overhead vs. the $2–$5 price — keep collection cheap.
