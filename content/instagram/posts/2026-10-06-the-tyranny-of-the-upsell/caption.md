An inclusive fare isn't mainly about money. It's about how many small decisions a week asks of you.

Count them on a fare built to be added to: the drinks package at embarkation, the cover charge at dinner, the spa tier, the internet plan, the wine by the glass or the bottle, the tour desk queue at four o'clock. None of them is expensive. Together they are a week of tiny negotiations with your own holiday.

Remove them and something odd happens. You order the second coffee. You try the restaurant you'd have skipped. You stop doing arithmetic at the bar. Nothing has been upgraded — the accounting has just gone quiet.

That silence is most of what people mean when they say a ship felt effortless.
