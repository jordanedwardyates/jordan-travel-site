Why it exists

A cruise fare is quoted per person but sold per cabin.

The ship has a fixed number of cabins and no more. One occupant instead of two halves the revenue from a room the line cannot re-cut.

Where it softens

Four places the supplement falls

01
Dedicated solo cabins
Small ships increasingly build a handful. Very few per sailing, and they go first — book them a season out.
02
Reduced-supplement promotions
Lines run them on sailings that need help. River cruises in shoulder months are the reliable hunting ground.
03
River rather than ocean
Solo terms on European river itineraries are generally kinder than on comparable ocean sailings.
04
The quiet season
Late autumn on the Danube, the Aegean before the crowds. Fewer couples competing for the same cabin.

How to read a solo quote

Four things to check

The multiplier
Quoted as a percentage of the double fare. It varies widely by line and sailing.
What it covers
Some supplements exclude port fees and taxes, some include them.
Category limits
The kind terms often apply only to the lower cabin grades.
Included extras
Ask whether credit and inclusions are per cabin or per guest. It matters most solo.
