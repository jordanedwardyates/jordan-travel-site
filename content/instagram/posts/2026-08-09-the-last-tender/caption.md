At a tender port there are two deadlines. Most travellers only read one of them.

All-aboard closes the gangway. The last tender leaves the shore earlier — a separate, earlier time, and a loaded tender still needs twenty minutes to cross. In the Aegean and in Alaska, moderate swell is enough to suspend tendering entirely, which is how a ship ends up anchored in full view of a port nobody gets to visit.

The quiet advantage of a smaller ship is that it berths alongside where a large one must anchor. Fewer tenders, fewer lost calls.
