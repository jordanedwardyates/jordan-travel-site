The best thing about the best ships is a list of things that never happen.

No announcements. No queue at the gangway. No photographer at dinner.

Absence is hard to sell, which is why nobody sells it. A brochure can show you a suite; it cannot show you the public address system staying silent all morning, or a tender that leaves when it is full rather than when it is scheduled, or a pool deck with no one on the microphone.

But absence is what people describe afterwards. They rarely mention the marble. They mention that nothing was ever being sold to them, and that they slept.

If you're weighing an Aegean week for next autumn, weigh what the ship refuses to do. Restraint is the specification, and it costs the line real money.

Save this for the next time you're comparing two ships.
