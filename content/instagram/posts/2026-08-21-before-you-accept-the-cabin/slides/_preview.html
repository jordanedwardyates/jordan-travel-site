Why the timing matters

Cabin choice is the one decision that gets worse every week you wait.

Ships, fares and itineraries stay available for months. Position does not. The quiet midship cabins go first, and they go to whoever asked for them by name.

Ask these, in this order

Five questions

01
What is directly above and below?
Pool deck, galley, lounge, crew stair. Any of the four is a reason to move one deck.
02
Does it have a connecting door?
You inherit whoever is behind it. On the plan it is a small mark that is easy to miss.
03
Is the view qualified in any way?
Obstructed, partially obstructed, or overhung. All three are disclosed, in one quiet word.
04
How far along the hull is it?
Forward for the drama, midship for the sleep. Aft if the water on that route is kind.
05
Is it an accessible cabin?
Wider doors, a roll-in bathroom, and often a better position. Do not book one you don’t need.

The accessible-cabin point

Held for a reason

If you need it
  • Ask on the first call
  • Confirm turning space
  • Confirm the bathroom type
  • Check the route to the lift
  • Get it in writing
If you don't
  • Leave it on the plan
  • Someone else cannot flex
  • Lines may reassign you
  • The saving is nil
  • It is simply bad manners

Timing, plainly

When position disappears

Twelve months out
The plan is open. Every quiet cabin and every good aft terrace is available.
Six months out
Midship goes first, then the accessible cabins, then the aft corners.
Inside ninety days
What remains is the guarantee pool, and the guarantee pool is what nobody chose.
Final fortnight
Fares may fall. Position will not come back. That is the whole trade.

The payoff

Book the ship early for the cabin, not for the fare.

A fare can be watched and repriced after you book — I do it routinely. A cabin position cannot be recovered once the deck plan has filled. Take the position; chase the price after.
