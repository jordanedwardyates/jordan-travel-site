The most expensive mistake in cruise planning isn't the ship or the fare — it's the cabin, chosen from a price list.

Category letters tell you what a cabin costs. The deck plan tells you what it will be like to sleep in. Those are different questions, and only one of them is answered on the booking page.

On a Mediterranean sailing with early tender mornings, a forward cabin near the anchor gear will wake you before Santorini has warmed up. A cabin under the pool deck loses you every sea day at six. Midship, two decks below the top, is where the quiet lives — and it rarely costs more.
