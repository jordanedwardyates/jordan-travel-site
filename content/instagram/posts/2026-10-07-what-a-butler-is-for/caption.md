Most people who get a butler at sea never work out what he is for — and spend the week feeling faintly awkward about it.

He is not there to pour things. He is a fixer with standing: the person who moves a dinner without you queueing, gets the pressing done before the captain's evening, arranges a quiet breakfast on the terrace at half six because you have an early tender at Katakolon, and knows which shore agent is worth ringing.

Used well, he removes logistics. Used badly, he is an expensive way to have champagne carried thirty feet.

So use him on the first afternoon. Tell him how you take your coffee, when you like to eat, and what you never want offered. Everything after that runs quietly, which is the whole idea.
