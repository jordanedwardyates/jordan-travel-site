Some ships are beautiful. Others were merely new once — and after eight years you can tell which at a glance.

Design that ages is made of materials that record use: oak that darkens, brass that dulls, leather that softens, wool that wears in rather than out. Design that dates is made of finishes that can only deteriorate — high-gloss lacquer, chrome, backlit acrylic, a mosaic feature wall in whatever colour that year liked.

The tell is the refit. A well-made room is refreshed. A fashionable one has to be replaced, and the replacement is chosen by the same instinct that got it wrong the first time.

Which is why I'll take a twenty-year-old ship with good bones over a new one dressed for a photograph. The sea is the view. The room only has to be quiet and well made.
