Good service is almost invisible. If you can see it happening, some of it is being performed at you.

The white-gloved line-up at the gangway, the folded-towel animal, the waiter who introduces the concept of the dish — that is theatre, and theatre is what a ship does when it cannot afford memory.

Memory is the expensive version. A barman who has your coffee right on day two. A stewardess who moves the reading lamp because she watched where you sat. Nobody mentions it. Nothing is announced. You simply stop having to ask for things.

That takes crew who stay — low turnover, long contracts, and enough of them per guest to notice anything at all. It is the least photogenic line in any brochure and the one I weigh most.
