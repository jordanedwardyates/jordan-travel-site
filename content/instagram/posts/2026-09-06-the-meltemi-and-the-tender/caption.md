The wind that cancels a Greek islands port day has a name, a season, and a time of day.

The meltemi is a dry northerly that funnels down the Aegean through July and August, and it is strongest in the afternoons. It is why a glassy morning becomes a lively afternoon at anchor — and occasionally why a tender port is swapped at short notice.

Tendering is the fragile link in any Mediterranean itinerary. Moderate swell or wind is enough to suspend it, and a ship anchored in sight of an island is still a missed port. Itineraries that berth alongside, and small ships that can berth where large ones must anchor, are structurally more reliable. That is not marketing.
