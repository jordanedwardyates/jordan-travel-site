Two shapes of sailing almost nobody searches for: the repositioning voyage and the back-to-back.

Ships move between seasons every spring and autumn - transatlantic, Mediterranean to Caribbean, Alaska to Asia. Those voyages sail long, with a run of open-ocean sea days and fewer, deeper port calls. The catch is honest: you need open-jaw flights, and the ship's clock advances about an hour a night eastbound.

A back-to-back is the other one worth knowing. Two consecutive loops, one embarkation city, the same cabin, and a quiet turnaround day whilst everyone else disembarks.
