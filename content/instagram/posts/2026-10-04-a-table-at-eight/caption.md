The test of a ship's dining isn't the menu. It's whether you can walk in at eight without planning it in March.

Reservation windows, tiered specialty covers, a concierge who can find you a table your neighbour couldn't — all of that is scarcity being managed, and scarcity onboard is a design decision, not a fact of the sea.

The ships I return to have fewer restaurants and enough seats. You come down when you're hungry. Nobody asks for your cabin number at the door. The maître d' remembers that you sit by the window.

On a small ship in the Adriatic in late September, dinner is a decision you make at seven forty-five. That is the luxury. It just doesn't photograph.
