Both formats are right. Neither is right for everyone.

I have talked people out of a river week and out of an ocean week in the same afternoon. The mistake is assuming the word cruise describes one kind of holiday.

Stay on the ocean

Four travellers I steer away from a river

01
The sea-day loyalist
No day off, no lounger afternoon, no long empty passage. The pace simply never lets up.
02
The family of six
One dining room and one lounge cannot serve six ages at once. An ocean ship can.
03
The space buyer
Locks cap the dimensions of the hull. If the room is the point, a river will disappoint.
04
The reluctant socialiser
The same hundred guests all week is either a feature or a trial. Be honest about which.

Never going back

Three who won’t return to ocean

01
The town-centre walker
Once you’ve walked off into Budapest after dinner, a berth two miles out feels like a tax.
02
The one-good-table diner
No booking game, no venue strategy. One kitchen, one room, every night of the week.
03
The unpackers
Vienna, Bratislava, Budapest, and the suitcase never moves. That is hard to give up.

The short version

Which week are you buying

Choose river
  • The destination is the point
  • You want mornings in towns
  • A small, quiet ship comforts
  • You like a planned day
Choose ocean
  • The ship is half the holiday
  • You want range in one week
  • Sea days are the reward
  • Mixed ages, mixed appetites

The payoff

The format is the decision. The line and the ship come after it.

Get river-or-ocean right and almost any well-run vessel will do. Get it wrong and the best ship on the water is still the wrong week.
