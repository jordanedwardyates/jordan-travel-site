Embarkation queues are arithmetic, not malice — and they are often self-inflicted.

Terminals stagger arrival windows because staggering is the only throughput tool they have. Ignoring yours puts you in a hall with people whose turn has not come, in a port that may be handling three ships that morning. Take your window, and take the quieter half of it.

The other delay is the confiscation bin. Surge-protected power strips, irons and travel kettles are commonly seized at screening, and every flagged bag becomes a hand search. Lists vary by line and by ship, so read yours before you pack.

And keep passports and documents in the bag that walks through the terminal with you.
