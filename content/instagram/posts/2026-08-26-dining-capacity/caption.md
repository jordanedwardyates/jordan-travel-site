The number of restaurants on a ship tells you very little. The number of seats tells you everything.

Cabins are straightforward to add to a design. Dining seats are not — which is why a much-praised ship can feel crowded at eight in the evening and deserted at three in the afternoon. Ten venues is not a choice if most of them are small.

Two honest signals: how early specialty reservations open, and whether there is a cover charge. An early window and a surcharge are both rationing. Genuinely open dining is a statement about capacity, and it is the thing I look for first on a Mediterranean sailing in high summer.
