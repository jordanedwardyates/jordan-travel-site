Do the arithmetic first

Take an hour off each end before you plan anything.

The first hour ashore is queue. The last hour is all-aboard, which sits thirty to sixty minutes before departure. What remains is the day you actually have.

One honest port day

08:00 alongside, 17:00 away

08:00 — alongside
Do not join the first crush. Coffee on deck, and walk off at nine into an emptier town.
09:00 — ashore
The two hours before the coach parties arrive are the only quiet ones you will get.
12:00 — the one thing
One site, properly. Two sites is a coach tour with your name on it, not a day.
13:30 — lunch inland
Ten minutes’ walk from the waterfront halves the price and doubles the cooking.
15:00 — turn around
Start back whatever you are mid-way through. The margin is the point of the plan.
16:00 — all-aboard
Aboard with an hour spare, on deck, watching the lines come in. That is the reward.

Where the hours vanish

Three thefts of a port day

01
Leaving with the first wave
Everyone tries to be first ashore, so nobody is. Twenty minutes’ patience buys you a clear terminal.
02
Two sights in one call
The second one is always rushed, and the rushing is what you will remember of both.
03
Lunch on the harbour front
The most expensive and least interesting meal in any Mediterranean port is the one facing your ship.

The timing nobody mentions

In July, arrive early or arrive in October.

Over-touristed calls are a scheduling problem before they are a crowd problem. Two ships alongside at ten means the old town belongs to you at nine, and to nobody at noon.
