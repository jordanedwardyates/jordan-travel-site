Sail north in summer and you aren't really booking weather. You're booking light.

Above the Arctic Circle around midsummer the sun doesn't set, and the whole rhythm of the day changes: dinner at nine still reads as afternoon, and the fjords hold a low, level glow for hours. That is the actual product of a June or early-July northern voyage, and no brochure photograph explains it properly.

Push either side of midsummer and the trade is explicit. Late May and early June bring meltwater in the falls and long days without the full midnight sun. Late August and September bring darkness back — which is the price of admission for the first aurora nights.

Northern summer and Aegean summer are opposite problems. One is about heat and crowds; the other is about how many hours of usable light you're buying.
