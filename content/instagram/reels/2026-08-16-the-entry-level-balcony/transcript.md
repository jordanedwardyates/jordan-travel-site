# The entry-level balcony

**Format:** critique · **Runtime:** ~40s · **Words:** 102 (at 2.6 w/s)
**Hook (first 3s):** The cheapest balcony on the ship is the one I refuse most often.

## Read-aloud script

> [0:00 — frame 01] The cheapest balcony on the ship is the one I refuse most often.
>
> [0:03 — frame 02] It's sold as a veranda. It's actually a low shelf with another deck built out over the top.
>
> [0:07 — frame 03] No sky, no sun, and the light of a car park.
>
> [0:12 — frame 04] At promenade level you inherit the lifeboat davits, and a crew working there at six.
>
> [0:17 — frame 05] The premium over a plain ocean view is real money.
>
> [0:22 — frame 06] So, honestly: if the balcony is why you're going, buy one with sky above it.
>
> [0:28 — frame 07] If it isn't, take a quiet midship ocean view.
>
> [0:34 — frame 08] Same fare, better sleep, and the difference goes ashore with you.

## Delivery notes
- Open flat and unhurried — the refusal is the hook, so don't sell it.
- Land "the light of a car park" dry, then a half-beat of silence before the davits line.
- Lift slightly at "so, honestly" — the critique has to resolve into advice, not grievance.
- Final line is warm. You're not scolding anyone; you're handing them the better cabin.
