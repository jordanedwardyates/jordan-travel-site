# The itinerary that looks perfect until you read the times

**Format:** critique · **Runtime:** ~48s · **Words:** 125 (at 2.6 w/s)
**Hook (first 3s):** Here's a seven-night Aegean itinerary that looks perfect. Seven ports, no repeats. Read the second column.

## Read-aloud script

> [0:00 — frame 01] Here's a seven-night Aegean itinerary that looks perfect. Seven ports, no repeats. Read the second column.
>
> [0:05 — frame 02] Two calls arrive at seven in the morning and leave at one. Not a day ashore.
>
> [0:10 — frame 03] Deduct all-aboard: thirty to sixty minutes before departure, in ship's time, not your phone's.
>
> [0:16 — frame 04] Two more are tender ports. The last tender back is an earlier, separate time - and swell can cancel it entirely.
>
> [0:23 — frame 05] One port sits two hours from the town it's named after. Four of six hours on a motorway.
>
> [0:30 — frame 06] So: seven ports, two of them visits. The rest are berths with names attached.
>
> [0:36 — frame 07] The version I'd book: four ports, one overnight, a sea day mid-week.
>
> [0:42 — frame 08] Same week, usually the same money. Send me an itinerary and I'll read the clock.

## Delivery notes
- Open flat and unimpressed - this is a reading, not a rant.
- Land 'that is not a day ashore' dry, then leave a full beat before the all-aboard line.
- The tender line is the turn: slow down, it's the part people don't know.
- Warm up for the last two beats. The verdict should sound like relief, not a close.
