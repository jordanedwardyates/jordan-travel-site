# The spa pass is a funnel

**Format:** critique · **Runtime:** ~50s · **Words:** 130 (at 2.6 w/s)
**Hook (first 3s):** The tiered spa pass isn't a luxury amenity. It's an upsell funnel with heated stone in it.

## Read-aloud script

> [0:00 — frame 01] The tiered spa pass isn't a luxury amenity. It's an upsell funnel with heated stone in it.
>
> [0:03 — frame 02] Here's the design. The hot loungers and the salt room get walled off from the spa you already paid for. A velvet rope inside the ship.
>
> [0:10 — frame 03] Then it's tiered. A day pass, a three-day, a full-voyage pass.
>
> [0:16 — frame 04] And the middle tier isn't really for sale. It's there to make the voyage pass look like the sensible choice.
>
> [0:23 — frame 05] That isn't pricing. That's decision architecture.
>
> [0:29 — frame 06] Then they'll ask you again on day two at the desk, because somebody has a target on it.
>
> [0:35 — frame 07] The ships I actually rate don't do any of this. The quiet room is just a room, and it's open.
>
> [0:43 — frame 08] Restraint costs them revenue. Which is precisely why it reads as luxury.

## Delivery notes
- Flat and unhurried. This is diagnosis, not outrage — the drier it is read, the more damning it lands.
- Half a beat before "decision architecture", and let it sit. That is the line people will send on.
- Take the last two lines slower and quieter than everything before them. No emphasis on "luxury" — the word does the work unstressed.
- Do not smile on the final line. Face to camera, still.
