# Footage — four ways to ruin embarkation day

**Reconstruction, not repost.** There is a great deal of passenger-shot embarkation and pier-runner
footage circulating. None of it is usable: it is filmed and owned by individual passengers, no
cruise line can license it, and Jordan's advisor permissions do not extend to it. Every beat below
is either shot by Jordan or genuinely licence-free. Nothing comes from social media.

**Loop discipline:** frames 01 and 08 must be the *same shot*, not two takes of it. Film the opening
clip long, then cut the head of it back onto the tail so the loop is seamless.

## Shot list

- **01 · 0:00 · Jordan to camera, 3s** — half-length, indoors, cream wall, no ship in frame. One
  sentence, then still. *Source: Jordan. Rights: own footage.*
  Keep the handle of a small leather holdall visible on the table beside him — it becomes the
  visual argument of the whole reel.
- **02 · 0:03 · Insert, 2s** — a blister pack and a paper pharmacy bag going into a suitcase, then
  the case zipping shut. Shot flat on a bed, natural light. *Source: Jordan. Rights: own footage.*
  No prescription text legible: use an unbranded box, or blur the label in post.
- **03 · 0:05 · Insert, 2s** — the same items lifted back out and dropped into the shoulder bag.
  Mirror the framing of 02 exactly so the correction reads instantly. *Source: Jordan.*
- **04 · 0:07 · Insert, 3s** — a coiled surge-protected power strip on a folded shirt, top-down,
  static. *Source: Jordan. Rights: own footage.* Do not film in or near a terminal.
- **05 · 0:10 · Paper insert, 2s** — a printed sheet on a desk, a hand ticking one line. Never show
  a line's logo or a screenshot of its website. *Source: Jordan. Rights: own footage.*
- **06 · 0:12 · Insert, 3s** — a passport wallet set deliberately *on top of* the closed suitcase,
  then picked up again. *Source: Jordan.* Keep the data page covered.
- **07 · 0:15 · Static exterior, 3s** — an empty quayside, bollards and mooring lines, mid-morning,
  no vessel name legible. *Source: Jordan, or Pexels — search "empty harbour quay bollard mooring
  rope". Rights: Pexels licence, royalty-free commercial, no attribution required.*
  Never use footage of a recognisable named ship — that is a brand and a compliance problem both.
- **08 · 0:18 · Jordan to camera, 3s** — the recycled head of clip 01. *Source: Jordan.*

## If line assets are wanted instead
Terminal or gangway b-roll may exist in the trade/advisor portal or through Jordan's BDM (Explora's
Travel Advisor Centre and Viking's agent tools are the two that reliably license for advisor use).
**Confirm with the BDM in writing before use** — press-centre assets are generally licensed for
editorial coverage *of the line*, not for an advisor's own brand promotion. Unconfirmed until then.

## Texture
For the paper grain behind the warn frames, pull a public-domain maritime chart or engraving from
the Library of Congress or Rijksmuseum collections. Both are genuinely public domain and suit the
printed-and-worn look far better than stock.
