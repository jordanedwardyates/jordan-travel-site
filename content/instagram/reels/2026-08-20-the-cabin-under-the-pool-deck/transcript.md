# The booking that fails on position alone

**Format:** post-mortem · **Runtime:** ~60s · **Words:** 155 (at 2.6 w/s)
**Hook (first 3s):** This is the booking that goes wrong most often, and it's never the ship's fault.

## Read-aloud script

> [0:00 — frame 01] This is the booking that goes wrong most often, and it's never the ship's fault.
>
> [0:03 — frame 02] The pattern is always the same. Somebody books late, takes the assigned cabin, and saves a little on the fare.
>
> [0:08 — frame 03] They land high, and right forward, on a fortnight with real open water in it.
>
> [0:14 — frame 04] Night one is fine. Night three the sea gets up, and forward is the end of a lever.
>
> [0:20 — frame 05] Then the anchor, in a tender port, at dawn, more or less under the bedhead.
>
> [0:27 — frame 06] By the fourth morning they're dozing in the library chairs and living on dry toast.
>
> [0:34 — frame 07] And there's nowhere to move them, because the ship is full. There never is.
>
> [0:41 — frame 08] Nothing was wrong with the ship, or the line, or the fare. The position was wrong.
>
> [0:48 — frame 09] Midship, two decks down, would have cost a little more and changed the entire fortnight.
>
> [0:55 — frame 10] So book early for the cabin. The fare I can chase afterwards.

## Delivery notes
- Flat and factual, the way you'd read back a report. No relish — this happens to careful people.
- Slow down through the anchor beat; it's the moment the audience recognises itself.
- "There never is" lands dry, then a full stop of silence before the finding.
- Warm on the final two lines. The point is that it was preventable, cheaply.
- Nobody is named, nothing is quoted. This is a recurring pattern, and it should sound like one.
