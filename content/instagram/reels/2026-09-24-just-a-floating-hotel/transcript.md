# "A river cruise is just a floating hotel"

**Format:** reaction · **Runtime:** ~49s · **Words:** 128 (at 2.6 w/s)
**Hook (first 3s):** Someone told me last week that a river cruise is just a floating hotel.

## Read-aloud script

> [0:00 — frame 01] Someone told me last week that a river cruise is just a floating hotel.
>
> [0:03 — frame 02] Honestly? Half right. And the half they meant as an insult is the good half.
>
> [0:08 — frame 03] It is a small hotel — a hundred-odd guests, one dining room, a crew who know your name by Tuesday.
>
> [0:15 — frame 04] The difference is that the hotel moves overnight and parks in the centre of the next town.
>
> [0:21 — frame 05] You unpack once. You walk off into Vienna, then Bratislava, then Budapest.
>
> [0:26 — frame 06] So no, it isn't a resort, and it isn't trying to be. No theatre, no second show at ten, no water slide.
>
> [0:33 — frame 07] If that absence sounds like a loss, take an ocean ship — genuinely, they're wonderful.
>
> [0:40 — frame 08] If it sounds like a relief, I know exactly which river you want.

## Delivery notes
- Amused, not defensive. The line was fair and you're conceding most of it.
- "Honestly? Half right." — real pause after the question mark.
- List the three cities evenly, unhurried; it should sound like remembering, not selling.
- The two closing sentences are a matched pair. Same rhythm, opposite verdict. Don't rush the turn.
