# The flight cancels, not the cruise

**Format:** post-mortem · **Runtime:** ~66s · **Words:** 175 (at 2.6 w/s)
**Hook (first 3s):** A flight cancels the night before a cruise. This happens every season.

## Read-aloud script

> [0:00 — frame 01] A flight cancels the night before a cruise. This happens every season.
>
> [0:03 — frame 02] It's the most common way people miss a ship.
>
> [0:07 — frame 03] The pattern is the same every time. Late evening, an airline app, a rebooking three days out, a sailing that leaves at dawn.
>
> [0:14 — frame 04] Booked direct, that's your problem to solve at an airport desk at midnight.
>
> [0:20 — frame 05] What solved it, every time I've seen it, was a call to the line before the terminal opened — so the cabin was flagged, not released.
>
> [0:28 — frame 06] Then new routing, a transfer moved, and a hotel for the night nobody planned on.
>
> [0:34 — frame 07] Sometimes the answer is joining at the second port, and the line arranges it because someone asked properly.
>
> [0:41 — frame 08] None of that is a discount. It's a phone call from a name the line recognises.
>
> [0:47 — frame 09] It's also why I tell every client to fly in a day early. The margin is the whole trick.
>
> [0:54 — frame 10] The trips that end badly are almost always flight-delay trips, not late-tour trips.
>
> [1:01 — frame 11] Fly in early. And keep one number that answers at midnight.

## Delivery notes
- This is a recurring pattern, not one client's story — keep it in the plural. No names, ever.
- Frames 03 and 04 are the sinking feeling: slow down, drop the pitch.
- Lift at "before the terminal opened" — that is the turn, and the on-screen ground flips with it.
- "None of that is a discount" is the thesis. Pause before it, land it flat.
- Last line: two short sentences, a beat between them, then stop. No sign-off flourish.
