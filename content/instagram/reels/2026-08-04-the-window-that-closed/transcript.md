# The window that closes when you pay early

**Format:** post-mortem · **Runtime:** ~67s · **Words:** 174 (at 2.6 w/s)
**Hook (first 3s):** "Here's the most expensive thing you can do with a cruise fare: book it and then stop looking."

## Read-aloud script

> [0:00 — frame 01] Here's the most expensive thing you can do with a cruise fare: book it and then stop looking.
>
> [0:07 — frame 02] Post-mortem. Not on a person — on a pattern I see every wave season.
>
> [0:12 — frame 03] Someone finds a sailing they love. They book direct, take the cheaper non-refundable deposit because the dates are certain, and pay in full early to get it off the desk.
>
> [0:23 — frame 04] All reasonable. Here's where it goes wrong.
>
> [0:26 — frame 05] Fares move. Lines reprice sailings that aren't filling, add credit, sweeten the category above. And before final payment, a booking can often be moved onto the better terms.
>
> [0:37 — frame 06] Nobody rings to tell you. There is no department whose job it is to notice.
>
> [0:43 — frame 07] Paying in full the day you book feels tidy, and it closes that window early. That's the actual cost of tidiness.
>
> [0:51 — frame 08] So: pay the deposit, diarise final payment, and have someone check the fare between the two. That's a fortnightly glance, not a job.
>
> [1:00 — frame 09] When I hold a booking, that glance is mine. It costs you nothing — the line pays me either way.
