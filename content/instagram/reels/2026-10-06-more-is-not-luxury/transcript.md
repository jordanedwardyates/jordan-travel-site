# More is not the same as better

**Format:** reaction · **Runtime:** ~50s · **Words:** 140 (at 2.6 w/s)
**Hook (first 3s):** Someone told me this week that a ship with twenty restaurants is more luxurious than a ship with four.

## Read-aloud script

> [0:00 — frame 01] Someone told me this week that a ship with twenty restaurants is more luxurious than a ship with four.
>
> [0:03 — frame 02] I think that's exactly backwards, and here's why.
>
> [0:07 — frame 03] Twenty kitchens share one provisioning budget and one talent pool. So you get twenty adequate rooms and a lot of signage.
>
> [0:14 — frame 04] Four kitchens, properly staffed, means the fourth one is actually good.
>
> [0:20 — frame 05] And it compounds. More decks means more guests. More choice means a reservation system.
>
> [0:26 — frame 06] A reservation system means a table at nine forty-five, or nothing.
>
> [0:31 — frame 07] So what's sold to you as abundance is really just scale — and scale is what you were paying to get away from.
>
> [0:38 — frame 08] What I look for is the opposite. Fewer rooms, more space in each. Dinner at eight without planning it.
>
> [0:44 — frame 09] Luxury isn't more. It's less, done properly, with nobody standing in front of you.

## Delivery notes
- Open conversational, as though repeating something you heard at a table. No indignation.
- "Exactly backwards" is the pivot — say it plainly and then pause before the reasoning.
- Land "nine forty-five, or nothing" completely dry. That's the laugh, and it should not be helped along.
- Warm on the last two lines. This is the advisor part, not the critic part.
