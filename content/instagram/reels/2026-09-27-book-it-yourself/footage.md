# Footage plan — "You can book it yourself"

Face for the first three seconds, b-roll through the middle, face again for the verdict.
Reconstruct, never repost: no passenger UGC, no named ship used to stand in for a voyage we sell.

## Shot list

**01 · 0:00–0:03 · Jordan to camera (face)**
Source: Jordan shoots himself. Desk, cream paper background, daylight from the left.
Rights: own footage. Deliver the hook flat, no lean-in.

**02–03 · 0:03–0:11 · Two fares on paper (b-roll, own)**
Source: Jordan shoots himself — two printed fare summaries side by side on the desk, a hand
moving between them, pencil circling a line. Print two real quotes with client details removed.
Rights: own footage. This is the visual argument; keep it static and slow.

**04–05 · 0:11–0:22 · Ledger / envelope insert (b-roll, own)**
Source: Jordan shoots himself — a fountain pen ruling a line in a notebook, then an envelope
being set down. Stands in for "the money is already in the fare" without a graphic.
Rights: own footage.
Alternative if time is short: licence-free stock, Pexels search "old ledger writing pen close up"
(https://www.pexels.com/license/) — no branded material, no identifiable faces.

**06–07 · 0:22–0:32 · Harbour texture (b-roll)**
Source: Jordan's own harbour footage first choice — quayside bollard, rope, water moving.
Fallback: Pexels or Coverr, search "harbour water morning slow" / "quay rope close up".
Rights: Pexels and Coverr are royalty-free for commercial use, no attribution required. Avoid any
identifiable branded ship in frame.
Do **not** request line-supplied assets for this reel — press-centre material is licensed for
editorial coverage of the line, not advisor brand promotion. If a line asset is wanted, confirm
with the BDM first.

**08–09 · 0:32–0:42 · Back to face (own)**
Same setup as frame 01 so the reel bookends. Final line delivered as an offer, not a pitch.

## Texture / stills (optional overlay)
Antique chart and engraving texture for frame backgrounds: Library of Congress maritime chart
collections and Rijksmuseum public-domain prints — both suit the printed-and-worn look better than
stock, and both are free of the stock-photo gloss problem. Confirm the specific item is
public domain before use.
