# Free drinks at sea, with the arithmetic put back in

**Format:** critique · **Runtime:** ~50s · **Words:** 130 (at 2.6 w/s)
**Hook (first 3s):** "Free drinks at sea — let's do the arithmetic the brochure won't."

## Read-aloud script

> [0:00 — frame 01] Free drinks at sea — let's do the arithmetic the brochure won't.
>
> [0:04 — frame 02] Nothing on that promotion is free. It's a fare with the discount pointed at the bar.
>
> [0:10 — frame 03] Here's the tell. The package has a daily value, and a service charge you pay on the value of the thing you were given for nothing.
>
> [0:20 — frame 04] Then there's the exclusion list. Premium pours, bottled water in the cabin, the good coffee. Read it.
>
> [0:27 — frame 05] And it's usually all adults in the cabin, whether or not the second one drinks.
>
> [0:33 — frame 06] None of that makes it a bad deal. It makes it a deal with your own drinking pattern in it.
>
> [0:41 — frame 07] So price it at what you'd actually order. Or sail a line where the question never comes up — that's what an inclusive fare actually buys.

## Delivery notes
- Dry throughout. This is a critique of a pricing structure, not of anyone who bought one.
- Land "for nothing" at 0:18 with a beat of silence after it — that is the whole joke.
- Slight warmth on the last sentence. The turn from complaint to alternative has to sound like relief, not a pitch.
- Face for 0:00–0:10, b-roll through the tells, back to face from 0:41.
