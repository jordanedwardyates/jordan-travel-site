# The worst port-day plan I was sent this year

**Format:** reaction / critique · **Runtime:** ~50s · **Words:** 130 (at 2.6 w/s)
**Hook (first 3s):** "Two hours inland, on a tender day, in a private car."

The plan is real but deliberately unattributed and the port is not named — the
critique is of the decision, never of the traveller who made it.

## Read-aloud script

> [0:00 — frame 01] Two hours inland, on a tender day, in a private car.
>
> [0:03 — frame 02] Eight-hour call, on paper. And the ship was anchored offshore.
>
> [0:09 — frame 03] Four of those eight hours were road. So the day was really four hours long.
>
> [0:16 — frame 04] Then the number that wasn't in the plan at all — the last tender was half past four. All-aboard was half past five.
>
> [0:23 — frame 05] Which makes the real margin fifteen minutes, before anyone queues for a tender.
>
> [0:30 — frame 06] And my objection isn't the guide. The guide was excellent. It's the arithmetic underneath him.
>
> [0:37 — frame 07] Same site, booked through the ship: if it runs late, the line owns it. It waits, or it moves you to the next port at its own cost.
>
> [0:44 — frame 08] Keep the private guide for the long, walkable call. That's where it's worth every penny.

## Delivery notes
- Open flat, almost bored — the hook works because you sound unsurprised.
- "Half past four" and "half past five" are the pivot. Pause before each; let the
  gap between the two numbers do the work.
- Frame 06 is the one warm beat: "the guide was excellent" should sound genuinely
  meant, not like a concession.
- Land frame 07 as fact, not triumph. No lift at the end of the sentence.
- Face for 0:00–0:09, b-roll through the middle, back to face from 0:37.
