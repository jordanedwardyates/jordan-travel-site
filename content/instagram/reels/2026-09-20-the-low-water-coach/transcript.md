# The low-water coach transfer nobody mentions

**Format:** critique · **Runtime:** ~48s · **Words:** 126 (at 2.6 w/s)
**Hook (first 3s):** Low water on the Rhine can turn a river cruise into a coach tour.

## Read-aloud script

> [0:00 — frame 01] Low water on the Rhine can turn a river cruise into a coach tour.
>
> [0:03 — frame 02] Let me be fair about it: water level is weather, and nobody controls the weather.
>
> [0:08 — frame 03] What I object to is where it gets disclosed.
>
> [0:12 — frame 04] It's in the contract. It is not in the brochure.
>
> [0:17 — frame 05] When a stretch runs too shallow to transit, the line reroutes — you coach to the next town, or two ships swap guests so neither has to cross it.
>
> [0:25 — frame 06] That can be handled beautifully. I've seen it done well.
>
> [0:30 — frame 07] But ask the questions at booking, not at the pier: what happens if levels drop, who funds a hotel night, is there a sister ship within reach.
>
> [0:38 — frame 08] Every line words that clause differently. That difference is the whole thing.

## Delivery notes
- Open flat and factual — no alarm in the voice. The claim is doing the work.
- Real pause after "nobody controls the weather." The fairness has to land before the critique.
- "It's in the contract. It is not in the brochure." — two clipped sentences, dry, no emphasis.
- The three questions at 0:30 read as a list, evenly, like dictation. Slow slightly.
- Last line lands quiet, almost an aside.
