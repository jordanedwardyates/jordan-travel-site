# Instagram Hashtag Research — Current Practice (2025–2026)

Researched 2026-07-30. For: luxury cruise / voyage advisory account, audience 45–70, affluent.
Positioning is *advisor and curator*, not booking engine — the hashtag bank below is filtered
for that. No influencer-bait, no volume-farming tags.

---

## 1. How many hashtags — the number

**Use 3–5. Never more than 5.**

Two things changed:

- **Instagram's own line changed first.** Adam Mosseri has stated repeatedly (2024–2025) that
  hashtags are *not* a reach mechanism: "Contrary to popular belief, hashtags are not a way to
  get more reach." His framing is that hashtags help Instagram *understand and index* content —
  they feed search and topic classification, not distribution.
- **Then the product changed.** As of **December 2025**, Instagram is reported to enforce a hard
  cap of **5 hashtags per post/Reel**, down from the historical 30. This is widely reported across
  social-marketing press (Social Media Today, Later, Manychat and others).
  *Verify before it matters commercially* — the cap's exact rollout state and whether it applies
  uniformly to Reels/carousels/Stories should be sanity-checked in-app before we build any
  templated caption system around it. The strategic conclusion (3–5) is unchanged either way,
  because it was already the recommendation before the cap.

Secondary signal worth knowing: piling on tags past ~5 has been read by practitioners as a
low-intent / spam signal. There is no upside case for 15–30 tags in 2026.

**Practical rule for this account:** 4 hashtags, placed at the end of the caption
(not in first comment — no measurable benefit, and it fragments the caption's keyword value).

## 2. Broad vs niche vs branded — the mix

With only 5 slots, the old "10 broad / 10 mid / 10 niche" ladders are dead. Ratio for a
4–5 tag post:

| Slot | Tier | Purpose | Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Mid niche** (100k–1M posts) | The actual category claim — "this is a luxury cruise post" | 1–2 |
| 2 | **Tight niche** (<100k posts) | Where our buyer actually browses; highest intent | 1–2 |
| 3 | **Branded / owned** | Owned surface, builds a searchable archive of our own work | 1 |
| — | **Broad** (>1M posts) | Use sparingly — at most 1, and only when genuinely descriptive | 0–1 |

Rationale: broad tags (#travel, #luxurytravel) are effectively noise at our follower size —
we will never surface in them, and they dilute the classification signal Instagram is reading.
The mid + tight combination is what tells the algorithm *who* to show us to. One branded tag on
every post is non-negotiable: it's the only tag we control and the only one that compounds.

Guideline for judging success: hashtag-attributed impressions running at ~5–10% of total reach
is normal and healthy in 2026. If we ever see hashtags carrying 30% of reach, something else is
wrong (usually: the content isn't being distributed on its own merits).

## 3. Do hashtags still drive reach? — What matters more

**No. Hashtags are an indexing/search input. Reach in 2026 comes from content signals.**

Ranked by what actually moves distribution:

1. **Sends / shares in DMs** — the single strongest signal Instagram has named publicly. Content
   worth forwarding to a spouse or travel companion. This is *extremely* favourable for our
   audience: "send this to whoever you're sailing with" is native behaviour for 45–70.
2. **Saves** — reference-value content. Advisor content is naturally save-heavy: port-day rules,
   cabin-deck logic, when-to-book windows.
3. **Watch time / retention** on Reels — the first 2 seconds and whether they stay.
4. **Social SEO — keyworded captions, on-screen text, alt text, and the account's name/bio
   fields.** This is where hashtag budget went. Instagram search now reads natural-language
   text. "Small-ship Mediterranean cruise for travellers who hate crowds" in a caption does more
   than any hashtag will.
5. **Hashtags** — categorisation, plus a modest search/browse tail.

**Implication for our workflow:** every post needs (a) a keyword-bearing first line, (b) a
keyword-bearing on-screen text frame, (c) real alt text, (d) 4 hashtags. Keywords carry the
discovery load; hashtags just file the post correctly.

Keyword phrases worth working into copy repeatedly (these are the search strings, not tags):
small ship cruise, luxury cruise advisor, Mediterranean cruise itinerary, Greek islands cruise,
Aegean sailing, river cruise Europe, expedition cruise, cruise cabin advice, all-inclusive
cruise fare, veranda vs suite, shore excursion planning, when to book a cruise.

## 4. Curated hashtag bank (~60)

Filtered: no #instatravel, #travelgram, #wanderlust, #travelblogger, #followme, #cruiselife
volume-farm tags. No tropical/party tags. Everything below is a real, populated tag a
prospective luxury cruise client or a peer advisor plausibly browses.

*Branded tags are placeholders pending Jordan's sign-off — pick two (one company, one editorial
series) and use them consistently forever. Their value is entirely in repetition. Do not rotate.*

### Ready-made 4-tag sets

- **Aegean / Greek islands post:** `#luxurycruise #greekislandcruise #aegeansailing #bonvtravel`
- **Small-ship advocacy post:** `#smallshipcruising #boutiquecruise #cruisewithoutcrowds #bonvtravel`
- **River cruise post:** `#rivercruise #danubecruise #slowtravel #bonvtravel`
- **Advisor / commentary post:** `#luxurytraveladvisor #cruisetips #cruiseplanning #bonvtravel`
- **Cabin / fare explainer:** `#cruisetips #verandasuite #whentobook #bonvtravel`
- **Line-specific spotlight:** `#luxurycruise #<lineTag> #smallshipcruise #bonvtravel`

### Hygiene rules
- Rotate within tier; never post the identical 4-tag block twice in a row.
- Check each tag in-app before first use — tags get restricted or hijacked; a restricted tag
  suppresses the post.
- Never tag a cruise line we can't credibly speak to, and never imply a brand partnership we
  don't have.
- Keep the tag block visually quiet: last line, after a line break. Paper-editorial voice means
  the tags should not be part of the read.
