# Instagram Reels — Research Notes (2025–2026)

Scope: talking-head and voice-over-over-b-roll advisory content, plus
critique / reaction / "here's what went wrong" content. Numbers below are the
published figures from the sources listed at the end; where sources disagree the
disagreement is preserved rather than averaged away.

Caveat on evidence quality: the only large-sample study in circulation is
Socialinsider's ~140,000-Reel analysis of business accounts. Most other numbers
are aggregated creator/agency benchmarks — directionally useful, not peer
reviewed. Treat the ranges as priors to A/B against our own retention curves.

---

## 1. Optimal length by format

Two different optima exist and they are not in conflict — they optimise
different metrics:

- **Discovery / completion / replays:** 7–30 s. 7–30 s Reels show the highest
  average completion rates, reported 20–35% higher retention than longer
  videos. Under 15 s gets up to 1.8x more replays than 60 s+.
- **Engagement rate and median views:** 45–60 s. In Socialinsider's ~140k-Reel
  2026 analysis, the 45–60 s bucket had the highest engagement rate of any
  length group and the highest median views (~10,374) — roughly 2x what sub-30 s
  or 3-minute-plus Reels managed.

Retention benchmarks by length (aggregated creator data):

| Length | Typical average retention |
|---|---|
| 7–15 s | 60–80% |
| 15–30 s | 40–60% |
| 30–45 s | 30–45% |
| 45 s+ | rarely above 30% unless exceptionally strong |

Recommended targets by our four formats:

| Format | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hook-driven listicle ("3 mistakes…") | **22–40 s** | ~6–10 s per item, 3 items max. Cut to 3 items rather than 5 to stay under 40 s. |
| Story / post-mortem ("what went wrong on…") | **50–75 s** | Needs setup→escalation→resolution. This is where the 45–60 s engagement bracket pays; 75 s is the practical ceiling. |
| Reaction / critique of a specific thing | **30–50 s** | One claim, one verdict, one reason. |
| Compilation / montage (b-roll + VO) | **12–25 s** | Loop-friendly; lives on replays, not completion depth. |

Governing rule from the sources: **retention, not duration, is the ranking
input.** A 20 s Reel watched to the end beats a 90 s Reel abandoned at 0:12.
Never pad to hit a bracket — trim to the shortest length that still lands the
payoff.

## 2. The hook window

- The scroll-or-stay decision is made in **~1.7 s** on average in 2026.
- Up to **50% of viewers drop in the first 3 seconds.**
- The algorithm's hook proxy is **3-second retention rate**; aim for **60%+**.
  Reels above 60% 3-s hold reportedly out-reach those below 40% by **5–10x**.
- One source claims measurement starts as early as **0.5 s** — so the hook must
  be in frame 1, not frame 3. No logo stings, no slow fades, no "hey guys."
- ~72% of viral Reels use a storytelling hook or a jump cut inside the first 3 s.
- Trending audio present in the first 3 s correlates with ~41% higher retention
  (use sparingly — must not fight the brand's quiet register).

3-second retention by hook category (aggregated 2026 benchmarks):

| Hook type | 3-s retention |
|---|---|
| Pattern interrupt | 72–84% |
| Curiosity gap | 65–78% |
| Direct question | 58–72% |
| Bold claim | 55–70% |
| Problem / pain | 50–65% |
| Social proof | 45–60% |

**Verbal hook formulas** (first 8–12 words; say the specific noun early):

1. Named-mistake: "The most expensive mistake on a Mediterranean sailing is…"
2. Negative list: "Three things I'd never book on a Greek-islands itinerary."
3. Contradiction: "The balcony cabin is the wrong upgrade. Here's what is."
4. Post-mortem cold open: "This sailing went wrong on day two — and it was
   avoidable."
5. Insider correction: "Every brochure says X. Every advisor knows Y."
6. Specific-number curiosity: "$4,200 for a cabin that has this view."
7. Stakes question: "Do you know what your cruise fare actually excludes?"
8. Verdict-first reaction: "This is the ship people keep asking me about. My
   answer is no."

**Visual hook formulas** (frame 1, before the first word finishes):

- Cold open on the *problem object*, not the destination (the obstructed-view
  window, the crowded tender queue, the fine-print line on a fare sheet).
- Motion or reveal on frame 1: a pan starting mid-move, a door opening, a page
  turning. Static establishing shots are the weakest openers.
- Text-on-frame-1 stating the claim in ≤6 words, so a muted viewer gets the hook.
- Face-then-cut: 1–1.5 s of direct-to-camera, then cut immediately to b-roll —
  the cut itself is the pattern interrupt.
- Deliberate visual mismatch: elegant archival/chart imagery against a blunt
  negative claim. On-brand and unexpected.

## 3. Retention and loop tactics

- **Captions/subtitles:** identical content with captions outperforms without by
  **40–60% in average watch time**; some creators report doubling. **85%+ of
  social video is watched muted.** Non-negotiable. Sync within **100 ms** of
  audio; word-level timing beats phrase-level. Sans-serif, high contrast, centre
  placement, **1–2 lines max**.
- **On-screen text cadence:** change the text every **1.5–3 s** (roughly every
  4–8 spoken words / every clause). Each cut should bring either a new line of
  text or a new visual — never both static for more than ~3 s.
- **Pattern interrupts every 3–5 s:** cut, zoom-punch, angle change, b-roll
  insert, on-screen annotation, or a beat of silence. Talking-head footage with
  no interrupt past ~5 s is where mid-reel drop-off concentrates.
- **Seamless loop:** end on the frame that visually matches the opening frame,
  and end the sentence one beat *before* the video ends so the first words read
  as the continuation. Loops inflate plays and watch time — most effective on
  the 12–25 s compilation format. Do not add "…so that's why" outro tails; they
  break the loop and lose the last 15%.
- **No dead outro:** cut the last frame at the payoff. CTAs belong in the
  caption or as a 1-line on-screen overlay during the final 2 s, not as spoken
  sign-off.

## 4. Why critique / mistake / reaction content over-performs

- **Negativity bias is neurological:** negative stimuli produce measurably more
  neural activity than equally intense positive stimuli, and negative social
  messages draw longer viewing times and more attention than positive ones in
  attention studies.
- **Reported effect sizes:** negative headlines get ~**30% more attention** and
  higher CTR; heavily negative/charged sources see ~**12% higher engagement**
  than balanced counterparts. Negative and moralised content propagates faster
  through networks than neutral or positive content.
- **"Avoid" beats "do":** negative-frame hooks ("what to avoid", "the mistake")
  win most A/B tests because they stack loss aversion on top of negativity bias.
  Number-framed negatives ("3 mistakes") show higher completion rates than
  positive equivalents.
- **Curiosity gap:** an unresolved question is aversive, so viewers stay for
  closure. "The one mistake killing X" works purely on incomplete information.
  Problem-Agitate structure is cited as fitting ~80% of educational content.
- **Travel/cruise specifics:** the live 2026 discourse is price-up/quality-down
  — fare-versus-value critique, nickel-and-diming, itinerary and cabin
  disappointments. This is exactly the negative territory where an advisor has
  standing to speak, and it converts because it reads as **fiduciary, not
  salesy** — the critique itself is the credential.
- **Brand guardrail:** the critique must land as connoisseurship, not
  cynicism — critique the decision, the fare structure, the design choice; never
  a named passenger, crew member, or client. Verdict must be followed by the
  better alternative inside the same Reel. Negativity buys the attention;
  the resolution is what earns the follow.

## 5. Voice-over reels specifically

- Conversational baseline: **125–150 wpm**. Short-form norm runs faster:
  **170–200 wpm** for high-energy delivery. Technical/complex material:
  **110–130 wpm**.
- **Our target: 150–165 wpm ≈ 2.5–2.75 words per second.** Script to
  **2.6 words/second**. Faster than that fights the brand's calm register;
  slower loses the scroll. Front-load the hook faster (~3 w/s for the first
  sentence), then settle.
- Practical script budgets at 2.6 w/s: 15 s ≈ 39 words · 30 s ≈ 78 ·
  45 s ≈ 117 · 60 s ≈ 156 · 75 s ≈ 195. Write to word count, not page count.
- Sentences of 8–14 words. One idea per sentence. Cut every clause that a
  visual already says.
- **Face vs b-roll:**
  - Frames 0–3 s: **face**, if there is one. A human face is the strongest
    3-second hold, and it establishes who is making the claim.
  - Body of the Reel: **b-roll**, with the face returning for the *opinion
    beats* — the verdict, the contrarian line, the recommendation. Sight of the
    speaker is what makes a judgement feel accountable.
  - Last 2–4 s: **face** for the verdict/CTA, unless the Reel is a seamless
    loop, in which case return to the opening b-roll frame instead.
  - Full-VO/no-face is acceptable for compilations and archival/chart-driven
    pieces; pure talking-head with no b-roll is the weakest configuration and
    should never run past ~30 s without an insert.

## 6. Format specs

- **Canvas:** 1080 × 1920 px, 9:16. Upload at 1080 wide minimum; export H.264,
  30 fps (or match source), high bitrate.
- **Safe zones on the 1080 × 1920 frame:**
  - **Top ~200 px** — account name, follow button.
  - **Bottom ~280–400 px** — caption text, audio attribution. Sources vary:
    ~400 px on the right side, ~270 px on the left. Treat **bottom 400 px** as
    unsafe for anything load-bearing.
  - **Right ~90–150 px** — like/comment/share/remix column.
  - Practical rule: keep all text, faces, logos and CTAs inside the **central
    70–80%** of the frame — i.e. avoid the top 20%, bottom 25%, right 15%.
  - Burned-in subtitles: place around **55–65% of frame height** (vertically
    centred-low), clear of both the bottom 400 px and the face.
- **Cover frame:** upload a purpose-built 1080 × 1920 cover rather than letting
  Instagram auto-pick. Feed crops to **4:5**; profile grid crops to **1:1 / 3:4**.
  So the message must survive the **centre 1080 × 1080** square; keep text and
  faces inside the centre **1080 × 1350** at minimum. Covers shrink in the grid —
  strong contrast, one clear focal point, minimal words (≤4). Soft type and low
  contrast disappear at grid scale.
- Design the cover as a piece of the grid, not just a thumbnail: the profile
  grid is the brand's contact sheet.
