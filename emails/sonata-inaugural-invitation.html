Why Sonata

An entirely new class of ship — and you’d be among the first ever aboard

Sonata isn’t a refit or a repeat — Oceania built her as a fresh expression of the small-ship experience they’re known for: just 1,210 guests cared for by 779 crew (a rare 1.5-to-1 ratio), so there’s never a crowd and never a line. She is all-veranda — every room has its own balcony — with a reimagined suite collection designed around more light, openness and comfort.

The Finest Cuisine at Sea® — reimagined for a new ship

The food is the reason people sail Oceania, and Sonata raises it again. She debuts two brand-new restaurants: La Table par Maîtres Cuisiniers de France, an intimate chef’s table rooted in French gastronomy, and Nikkei Kitchen, a vivid Peruvian-Japanese room. The classics come with her, too — Polo Grill, Toscana, the Grand Dining Room, and the beloved Terrace Café — and every dish is cooked to order, never en masse.

Elsewhere: a new spirit to the bars, from a sunset botanical spritz in Horizons to a crafted cocktail at Founders Bar; and at the top of the ship, two headline accommodations making their debut on this class — the opulent Horizon Suite and the distinctive Penthouse Deluxe. More on the rooms in a moment.

The Penthouses · a note on the two

Both Penthouse categories come with the things that change a cruise: a 24-hour butler, priority reservations at every specialty restaurant, and an expanded veranda. The Penthouse Suite (PH2) is the entry into that butler-served world. The Penthouse Deluxe (PD1) is brand-new to this class — a larger, lighter, more open reimagining of the penthouse, the top of the penthouse floor before you reach the full Owner’s- and Vista-level suites. Think of PH2 as stepping into butler service, and PD1 as the best seat on that deck.
