Why not June 6

Viking’s itinerary ends with two nights in Prague, so a June 6 departure has you checking out of the Prague hotel on Thursday June 15. If you still head straight to Kraków that morning, you arrive on Corpus Christi, and it is worth knowing what that is. Sixty days after Easter, Catholic Europe carries the Host through the streets: Poland above all, but also Austria, Croatia, Portugal, the Catholic south and west of Germany (Bavaria and the Rhineland, not Berlin), Spain’s old cathedral cities such as Toledo and Seville, and the Catholic cantons of Switzerland. The Czech Republic does not keep it as a holiday, so Prague will be an ordinary Thursday. Poland does, and because it always falls on a Thursday, the country takes a four-day weekend.

Kraków’s procession is the grandest in Poland. It leaves Wawel Cathedral after morning Mass, stops at four altars built along Grodzka Street, and ends in the Main Square before the Basilica, with the archbishop, the city guilds, girls in white scattering petals, and Highlanders in costume. Elsewhere the famous ones are Łowicz, for the folk dress, and the village of Spycimierz, which lays a carpet of flowers a mile long for the Host to walk on. If you were standing in the square at eleven that morning it would be one of the sights of the trip.

You would not be. You would be on the road from Prague, arriving mid-afternoon to a city where the shops are closed by law, the restaurants near the square are taken by the long-weekend crowds, and the hotels are at their June peak. The only way to see it is to give up Viking’s second Prague night and reach Kraków on the 14th, which I would not trade. So the 1st clears the holiday on the front side, the 11th clears it on the back, and the 11th with one extra night gets you Wianki instead, the midsummer festival on the Vistula on Saturday the 24th, which asks nothing of you but to turn up.
